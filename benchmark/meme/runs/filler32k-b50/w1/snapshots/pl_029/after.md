Minimalist music is characterized by simple harmony, repetitive melodic patterns, slow harmonic change, steady pulse, and layered repetition and development. It emerged in the US during the 1960s-70s as a reaction against the European avant-garde's complexity and atonality, influenced by social movements and non-Western musical traditions. Recognition grew in the 1980s-90s as minimalist music was integrated into film and television soundtracks. Despite skepticism about its repetitiveness and perceived simplicity, minimalist music's appeal lies in its emotional impact, accessibility, meditative qualities, and subtle structural and textural complexities such as phasing and harmonic variations.
